# Procedure

1. Restate the problem in the user's words. Separate the observed symptom from possible causes; ask why until the need is expressed as an outcome for people or the business. Label assumptions and keep unsupported causes open.
2. Identify who experiences the problem, when it occurs, and the relevant context. Record evidence and its source; distinguish observations from hypotheses.
3. Describe what would change for those people if the problem were solved. Agree signals of success before proposing a feature or choosing a stack.
4. Explore alternatives and their consequences, including a process change or doing nothing. Only then discuss possible features. Extract candidate domain rules with status `proposed` and candidate scenarios in plain language before committing to a feature list. Keep candidates in the brief, not in approved context rules or contracts.
5. A throwaway prototype or mockup may be used as a question tool only within explicitly assigned files/effects. Record the question, observations, resulting decision and approval source in the brief. The prototype is not evidence of approved semantics and never becomes implementation precedent or authority to begin product code.
6. Separate approved decisions from proposals and mandatory unknowns. Ask the domain owner for decisions needed for consolidation; missing mandatory semantics block dependent work.
7. Return the problem-first brief; hand off to the architect only after required approvals. Approval covers the recorded decisions, not unreviewed implications of an example or mockup.

Close with one outcome under the stage contract. Approval and assignment amendments are recorded by their issuer/dispatcher, not manufactured by this role.
